Welcome to my corner of the internet for country music. This started as a page about my favorite song, and it's turned into a running guide — the artists I always come back to, the tracks worth knowing, and where to catch them live. I add to it whenever something new earns a spot.

All-Time Favorite Song

"Courtesy of the Red, White and Blue"

Toby Keith

If I had to pick one song, it's this one — no contest. Proud, defiant, and unmistakably American, it's the song that gets played loudest on every road trip and never gets old.

The Story Behind the Song

Why "Courtesy of the Red, White and Blue" hits harder than any other track.

1

Written in 20 Minutes

Keith wrote the lyrics on the back of a fantasy football sheet in late 2001, under the working title "The Angry American." It came together in about twenty minutes — a rare case of a song arriving almost fully formed.

2

A Tribute to His Father

The song was inspired by Keith's father, Hubert "H.K." Covel Jr., an Army veteran who lost an eye while serving in the Korean War and flew the flag in their yard every day. His father died in a car accident in March 2001 — six months before 9/11. Keith wrote the song imagining his father's reaction to the attacks.

3

A No. 1 Hit — and a Firestorm

Released in May 2002 as the lead single from Unleashed, it hit No. 1 on the Hot Country Songs chart that Fourth of July. Its unapologetic tone also made it one of the most controversial country songs of its era — The Chicks' Natalie Maines publicly criticized it, sparking a very public feud with Keith.

4

Still Climbing, 24 Years Later

Around America's 250th birthday in July 2026, the song saw a massive resurgence — topping Spotify's charts for the Fourth of July and climbing to No. 11 on the Billboard Hot 100, the highest chart position any Toby Keith song has ever reached, more than two decades after it first came out.

Why It's My Favorite

It's proud, it's defiant, and it means something. Every time it comes on, it's the loudest sing-along of any road trip — and knowing the real story behind it, the tribute to Keith's father and the moment it came out of, only makes it hit harder.

Meet the Artists

A little background on the careers behind the songs — for anyone who wants the full story.

Zach Bryan

b. 1996

Oologah, Oklahoma

Before he was a chart-topping songwriter, Bryan spent eight years as an active-duty member of the U.S. Navy, writing and recording songs in his off-hours. He self-released his debut album, DeAnn, in 2019, and broke through nationally with the 2022 triple album American Heartbreak.

Career Highlights

  • Named ACM New Male Artist of the Year (2022)
  • His self-titled 2023 album debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard 200
  • Won the 2024 Grammy for Best Country Duo/Group Performance for "I Remember Everything" (feat. Kacey Musgraves), which also topped the Hot 100
  • Set the record for the largest ticketed concert in U.S. history — 112,408 fans at Michigan Stadium (September 2025)
  • Sixth album, With Heaven on Top, debuted at No. 1 in January 2026
  • Over 30 million records sold

New Album Spotlight — With Heaven on Top (Jan. 9, 2026): his sixth studio album, released via Belting Bronco Records and Warner Music Group, debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard 200. My favorite track off it is "Rivers and Creeks" — it's on repeat right now.

Morgan Wallen

b. 1993

Sneedville, Tennessee

Wallen first gained national attention as a contestant on Season 6 of The Voice in 2014. His debut album, If I Know Me (2018), introduced "Whiskey Glasses," but it was 2021's Dangerous: The Double Album that made him one of the biggest names in country music.

Career Highlights

  • One Thing at a Time (2023) produced "Last Night," his first Billboard Hot 100 No. 1
  • At one point held five of the top 10 spots on the Hot 100 simultaneously — a feat matched by only a handful of artists, including Taylor Swift and The Beatles
  • Won CMA Entertainer of the Year (2024)
  • Earned his first Grammy nominations in 2025 for "I Had Some Help"
  • I'm the Problem (2025) was his third consecutive chart-topping album
  • The highest RIAA-certified country artist in history, with over 265 million certified units

Toby Keith

1961 – 2024

Clinton, Oklahoma

Before music, Keith worked in the Oklahoma oil fields and played defensive end in the USFL. His self-titled 1994 debut went platinum, but it was 2002's Unleashed — home to "Courtesy of the Red, White and Blue," which he wrote in twenty minutes after 9/11 — that cemented his superstardom.

Career Highlights

  • 33 No. 1 hits and 42 top-10 hits across his career
  • More than 40 million albums sold, and over 10 billion career streams
  • Two-time ACM Entertainer of the Year (2002, 2003); 14-time ACM Award winner overall
  • Inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame (2015)
  • Received the National Medal of Arts (2021) and the BMI Icon Award (2022)
  • Posthumously inducted into the Country Music Hall of Fame (2024)

Toby Keith passed away in February 2024 after a two-year battle with stomach cancer. He was known off-stage for his USO tours entertaining U.S. troops overseas and his annual golf classic, which funded his charitable foundation — a legacy as generous as it was loud.

Cody Johnson

b. 1987

Sebastopol, Texas

Johnson built his career the hard way, independently releasing six albums — rooted in Texas rodeo and cowboy culture — before signing with Warner Music Nashville in 2018. That grassroots foundation is part of why his traditional country sound has connected so widely.

Career Highlights

  • "'Til You Can't" (2021) earned his first Grammy Award (Best Country Song) and first Country radio No. 1
  • Album Leather won CMA Album of the Year; "'Til You Can't" won CMA Single of the Year and Music Video of the Year
  • Won CMA Male Vocalist of the Year and the ACM Song of the Year (for "Dirt Cheap") in 2025
  • Won ACM Entertainer of the Year and Male Artist of the Year in May 2026
  • Set a concert attendance record at Houston's NRG Stadium — 80,000+ fans (March 2026)
  • Released Banks of the Trinity in June 2026, featuring Luke Combs and Brothers Osborne

Zac Brown Band

formed 2002

Atlanta, Georgia

Frontman Zac Brown (b. July 31, 1978) sold his restaurant in 2004 to buy a tour bus and build the band from the ground up. Their major-label debut, The Foundation (2008), blended country with rock, reggae, and bluegrass — a genre-blurring sound that became their signature.

Career Highlights

  • "Chicken Fried" (2008) was their debut single and first No. 1 hit
  • Three Grammy Awards, including Best New Artist (2010) and Best Country Album for Uncaged (2013)
  • Six consecutive albums in the Billboard 200 top 10; five straight No. 1s on the Top Country Albums chart
  • 16 No. 1 singles, over 30.5 million singles and 9.3 million albums sold, and more than 11.2 billion catalog streams
  • Zac Brown founded Camp Southern Ground, a camp bringing together neurodiverse children and youth facing social, emotional, or financial challenges with their peers
  • Received the inaugural Veterans Voice Award at the 2025 American Music Awards for his support of the military community

Jon Pardi

b. 1985

Dixon, California

Pardi moved to Nashville in 2008 and worked as a lifeguard while chasing a publishing deal — he landed one 18 months later, then signed with Capitol Records Nashville. His sound stayed rooted in traditional honky-tonk even as he became one of country's biggest headliners.

Career Highlights

  • Won the CMA New Artist of the Year and ACM New Male Vocalist of the Year, both in 2017
  • "Head Over Boots," "Dirt on My Boots," "Heartache Medication," and "Last Night Lonely" all topped the Billboard Country Airplay chart
  • California Sunrise (2016) went platinum
  • Mr. Saturday Night was nominated for ACM Album of the Year (2023)
  • Inducted into the Grand Ole Opry in 2023 — its first member from California

Toby Keith

In Memoriam

Final Shows · Dolby Live, Park MGM, Las Vegas · December 2023

Keith's touring days ended in 2021 as he focused on treatment for stomach cancer, but in December 2023 he took the stage for three sold-out "rehab shows" in Las Vegas — his way of getting back up one more time.

His very last performance, on December 14, 2023, closed with "Courtesy of the Red, White and Blue (The Angry American)" — featuring a cameo from comedian Carrot Top. He passed away less than two months later, on February 5, 2024. Fittingly, his final song was the same one that means the most here.
